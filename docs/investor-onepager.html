The problem

Why guessing costs them the sale

A seller who wants this done properly hires a listing consultant. That is expensive, slow, and impossible to repeat across a whole catalogue. So most sellers reach for software instead, and the software tells them to “add more keywords,” which nobody can actually act on.

Underneath both failures sits the same difficulty: what makes a listing good is different in every category. A supplement sells on ingredient quality and proof of testing. A skincare serum sells on what is in it, the evidence behind it, and how it feels on the skin. Software that judges both the same way is judging neither, which is exactly why generic tools give generic advice.

Why it holds

Why this is hard to copy

The clever technology is the easy part. Anyone can buy that. What is hard to copy is knowing what a good listing looks like in a particular market, and being able to show that our verdict is consistent and trustworthy.

Category expertise, written down

Each product category has its own standards, built by hand and weighted by what matters in that market. This is the accumulated judgement an expert consultant carries in their head, captured once and then applied to every seller in that category at no extra effort.

Judgement that is tested

The product’s promise is a verdict on someone’s listing, so the verdict itself is put under test: the same listing must be graded the same way twice, and strong listings must consistently outrank weak ones. That testing runs before every change ships.

It improves without rebuilding

Testing surfaced where inconsistent judgement comes from, and the fix turned out to be how the standards are worded, not how the software is built. Every category added from here is written correctly from the start, and gets sharper as the library grows.

Built to sell, not just to demo

Customer sign-ups, secure access and cost controls are already in place, so we can put this in front of paying customers rather than go back and build the business side from scratch.
